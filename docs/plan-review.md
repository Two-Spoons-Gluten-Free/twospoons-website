# Two Spoons — plan and website review

Reviewed September 27, 2026 against **Two Spoons Plan.docx**, June 2026, version 1.0. This is an internal implementation note; it is not website copy. The business is **pre-revenue**, as specified by the owner.

## What is strong

The father–daughter origin gives the brand a clear human story. Baking mixes, granola, and dried pasta make an understandable pantry concept. The plan sensibly emphasizes a small initial range, direct customer feedback, consistent recipes, and measured growth. The owner selected a playful family-kitchen direction: cheerful colors, illustrations, and the father–daughter story at the center. Simple ingredient language and the Sterling, Virginia connection can communicate the idea without publishing the operating plan.

The main editorial correction is tense: the document mixes intentions with statements that sound already accomplished. The website should say the collection is being developed and the business is preparing to launch. It should not imply existing customers, sales, market appearances, shipping capability, certifications, or completed kitchen controls.

## Website priorities

At least five distinct pages should give visitors a reason to explore:

1. **Home:** memorable food-first introduction, Sterling location, clear coming-soon status, and links into the collection and story.
2. **The pantry:** previews of the three planned categories, with the owner's requested **Buy Now** buttons leading to an order-request flow. Do not invent prices, package sizes, ingredients, or inventory. Let visitors request quantities of baking mixes, granola, and dried pasta; explain that Two Spoons will personally confirm prices and availability.
3. **Our story:** the father–daughter partnership and shared-table idea. Founder names and portraits require approved public assets.
4. **Our approach:** the intended sourcing, recipe development, and gluten-free practices, clearly expressed as work in progress.
5. **Launch / stay connected:** honest availability information and the owner's requested newsletter **Sign Up**. Save submissions on the server with clear consent and truthful confirmation. Actual local persistence is a valid collection mechanism; email delivery or subscriber export can be connected afterward.

The order form collects requests without payment. Its explanation and success message should make clear that submitting a request does not complete a paid or final order. Persist the request before reporting success, and give the visitor a reference or confirmation. Newsletter consent should remain separate from an order request.

An FAQ can additionally explain that launch timing, final recipes, allergens, and market dates will be announced when confirmed. Keep ownership, governance, succession, budgets, and other private operating details out of the public site. Illustrative imagery must not be presented as photographs of finished Two Spoons products.

## Factual corrections to the plan

- **Online sales are now allowed.** HB 402 expanded qualifying sales to internet/telephone channels and delivery to Virginia consumers by mail or delivery service. This is current law, rather than an upcoming July 2026 event. The official legislative summary confirms the change; the updated Code states the current limits. [Virginia Division of Legislative Services, 2026 changes, pp. 1–2](https://dls.virginia.gov/pubs/idc/idc26.pdf), [Va. Code § 3.2-5130(C)(3)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title3.2/chapter51/section3.2-5130/).

- **The mailbox section needs revision.** The current statute expressly permits a physical address **or post office box number**. A USPS PO Box is therefore not categorically uncertain under this exemption. The sources reviewed do not establish the plan's blanket claim that any commercial mailbox in a suite format “unambiguously” satisfies all address requirements; remove that assurance. [Va. Code § 3.2-5130(C)(3)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title3.2/chapter51/section3.2-5130/).

- **Exemption does not mean immunity from inspection.** The law exempts qualifying operations from specified permit, inspection, and inspection-fee requirements but preserves inspection following a consumer complaint. Sales and delivery remain limited to Virginia individuals for personal consumption, with no resale, consignment, or retail-food-establishment use. [Va. Code § 3.2-5130(C)(3), (D)](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title3.2/chapter51/section3.2-5130/).

- **Product categories are not automatic recipe approval.** VDACS lists dried pasta, dry baking mixes, and granola, but the finished product must not require temperature control for safety. Its July 2026 guidance says recipe review and sometimes laboratory assessment are needed. Validate the actual formulations and drying/storage process before describing the range as launch-ready. [VDACS Home Food Processing Exemptions FAQ, pp. 1–3](https://www.vdacs.virginia.gov/pdf/homefoodexemptionsfaq.pdf).

- **The label checklist is incomplete.** Add product identity and net quantity, and determine whether Nutrition Facts are required. Ingredients, subingredients, and applicable major allergens also need final, accurate declarations. VDACS requires net content in both standard and metric units. [VDACS Basic Labeling Requirements, pp. 1–4](https://www.vdacs.virginia.gov/pdf/basicfoodlabeling.pdf).

- **Gluten-free is a substantiated product claim.** FDA's definition includes ingredient restrictions as well as unavoidable gluten below 20 ppm. Certified inputs alone do not certify the finished products. Do not publish a certification badge, “zero gluten,” “celiac-safe,” or a claim of a fully dedicated facility without supporting evidence. The plan supports an intended process, not proof that it is already operating. [FDA gluten-free labeling questions and answers](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-food-labeling-and-critical-foods/questions-and-answers-gluten-free-food-labeling-final-rule).

## Information still needed before a sales launch

- Confirmed launch date and actual initial products, recipe specifications, allergen declarations, shelf life, package sizes, prices, and real product photographs.
- Confirmed public founder details, business contact information, domain, mailing address, and company-formation status; the plan describes formation both as completed and as future work.
- Evidence supporting the intended gluten-free process and the claims used on product pages and packaging.
- Confirmed market participation, pickup arrangements, Virginia delivery coverage, order lead times, and customer service/refund terms.
- Clear newsletter consent, a privacy notice matching actual data handling, and verified server persistence for subscriber and order-request records. Establish an operator retrieval/export process and connect email delivery before promising automated messages.

The prelaunch site can collect newsletter signups and unpaid order requests independently of a sales launch once its public contact details, assets, and data handling are ready. Confirm the actual products, prices, availability, and fulfillment arrangements with the customer before accepting an order as final. Collecting an order request does not establish that products are ready to sell or ship.
